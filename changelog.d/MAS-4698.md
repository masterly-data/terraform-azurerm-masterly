### Added

- **`split_processing`: the core and outbound Container Apps environments (ADR 0120 §4).** Off by default, so an upgrade plans no change. On, the module adds `aca-<name_prefix>-core` and `aca-<name_prefix>-outbound`, both internal, each on a subnet of its own (`snet-core` and `snet-outbound`, from `core_subnet_prefix` and `outbound_subnet_prefix` or the /24s at index 8 and 9 of a /20 or larger VNet; `core_subnet_id` and `outbound_subnet_id` on an injected network). Their NSGs carry the platform rules and the deny only, and the private-endpoint subnet admits both on the data-plane ports through two new rules (`allow-data-plane-from-core-subnet`, 140, and `allow-data-plane-from-outbound-subnet`, 150); no existing rule is renamed or renumbered. The environments are created empty: no app or job runs there yet, and the workers environment stays. Their egress is your firewall's to narrow: the module attaches no route table to them. See `docs/networking.md`, "The add-on environments".
- **`extensions`: an extension Container Apps environment of its own (ADR 0120 §4 and §7, ADR 0122 §6.2).** Off by default, so an upgrade plans no change. On, the module adds `aca-<name_prefix>-extension`, internal, with no certificate or Masterly identity, on `snet-extension` (`extension_subnet_prefix`, or the /24 at index 10 of a /20 or larger VNet; `extension_subnet_id` on an injected network). No allow rule the module writes names the extension subnet as a source, the private-endpoint subnet's above all. Because `snet-aca` and `snet-front` admit `VirtualNetwork` on an internal install, which covers the extension subnet, both NSGs gain `deny-from-extension-subnet` at priority 100 with the add-on on, and `allow-app-ingress` moves from 100 to 105 behind it; without the add-on neither rule changes. On an injected network keep its range off your endpoints subnet's sources, and deny it ahead of any rule of yours that admits `VirtualNetwork`.
- The `environment_subnet_ids`, `environment_subnet_prefixes` and `container_app_environments` outputs gain the core, outbound and extension keys only with their add-on on. The plan refuses an add-on whose subnets cannot be made, and a subnet input set with its add-on off.

### Changed

- **`environment_zone_redundant` now reaches the core and outbound environments** too, as the workers environment split in two (ADR 0120, 2026-11-20 amendment). The extension environment is never zone-redundant. The input's validation now asks for the front, app and workers environments rather than exactly three environments, so it plans with the add-ons on.
